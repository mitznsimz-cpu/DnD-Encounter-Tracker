Why This Tracker Exists

Running combat in D&D 5e requires a Dungeon Master to simultaneously manage initiative, HP, conditions, concentration, legendary actions, ability recharges, and round flow — all while narrating the scene and keeping players engaged.

The MitzNSimz Encounter Tracker removes that friction so the DM can focus on what matters: the story, the drama, and the players.

Core Design Principles: Offline First · No Accounts · No Subscriptions · Local Data · DM-Focused · Physical Dice

What this tracker is not: not a Virtual Tabletop (no grid or maps), not a character sheet manager, not a dice roller, not a campaign manager.

Best viewed on: a tablet (iPad, Android tablet) or laptop/desktop browser gives the most room. Phones are fully supported too (v1.37): the toolbar becomes a single side-scrolling row, and when you start combat the header automatically collapses to a slim bar showing the round, the active combatant and a Next button — handing the rest of the screen to the combatant list. Scrolling down also collapses it; to bring the full header and footer back, tap the small chevron on the slim bar (it stays collapsed while you scroll so nothing flickers in and out). You can collapse or expand the header at any time with the chevron next to ? Help.

Offline Use, Privacy & Data

Cookies

This tracker uses no cookies of any kind. No cookie consent is required. (Mitz & Simz, however, are always happy for treats. 🐾🐈‍⬛)

Privacy & GDPR

This tracker does not collect, store, transmit, or process any personal data. No user accounts, no analytics, no tracking, no third-party advertising. All data you enter — encounter names, combatant names, custom monsters, party configurations — is stored exclusively in your own browser's local storage (localStorage and IndexedDB) and never leaves your device.

The only external connection this tracker makes is to Open5e on first load to download the monster and spell database. No user data is included in that request. Once that download has completed and been cached, the tracker's combat features and the cached reference libraries work with no connection at all. (A completely fresh browser — or one whose storage has been cleared — needs one online visit to fetch the database again.)

The mini-shop link (v1.116): stat blocks in the Monster DB end with one optional "Looking for a mini?" link to the MitzNSimz shop. It is an ordinary link: nothing is sent anywhere unless you click it, and clicking it simply opens the shop's search page in a new tab. The only thing the shop receives is the search words shown in the link (e.g. "Goblin"). The tracker adds no tracking code, and the link is set so your browser doesn't even say which page you came from — the visit looks exactly as if you had searched the shop yourself. Once you're on the shop you're an ordinary visitor to that website, covered by its own privacy policy. Don't want it? ⋯ More → 🛒 Mini Shop Links: Off.

The tracker is designed to minimise privacy concerns: there are no accounts, no analytics, no third-party advertising and no server-side storage, and everything you enter stays in your browser on your own device. MitzNSimz does not collect, receive or process any of it — so there is nothing held about you for us to disclose, delete or export. Note that anything you type in (player names, for example) is still personal data on your device, and remains under your control: you are responsible for what you choose to enter and for any backup files you export. Clearing your browser storage removes it all.

Local Storage & Offline Use

Your encounter data never leaves your device. The tracker stores everything locally — no cloud, no account, no server.

You need to be online once. On first open the monster and spell database downloads (~30–60 seconds). After that the tracker runs entirely offline — no connection needed at the table. The only time you need to be online again is to update the database on demand, or to re-fetch it if browser storage has been cleared (or you start fresh on another device or browser).

⚠ Browser storage may be lost if storage is cleared, the device is replaced, or the browser is uninstalled. Export regularly as a backup.

Full Backup (recommended) (v1.33): ⋯ More → ⬇ Full Backup saves all your custom data — parties, encounters, monster groups, custom monsters and custom spells — in one file. ⬆ Restore Backup loads it back and asks whether to Merge (keep your current data, add anything new, skip duplicates) or Clear & Replace (wipe your current custom data first, then restore only the file). App preferences (theme, font, ruleset) are not part of the backup — re-set those after restoring.

Backup reminder (v1.40): under the Full Backup menu item, a small passive note shows when you last backed up and when your custom content last changed (e.g. “Last backup: 5 days ago · custom content changed today”). It’s just a gentle at-a-glance reminder — no popups, no nagging.

Storage used (v1.40): the Help panel’s Offline Use section shows roughly how much browser storage the app is using. The app also asks your browser to keep its data persistent, so the monster cache and your saved encounters are less likely to be cleared automatically when the device is low on space.

New content notice (v1.46): when you’re online, the tracker quietly asks Open5e how many creatures and spells it currently offers and compares that with your local cache (your custom monsters don’t count). If Open5e has grown, the caption under ↺ Refresh Monster DB gains a gentle note — e.g. “Open5e has 42 new monsters — refresh to sync.” A small gold ✦ badge also appears next to the database status in the header (with the count on wider screens); tap it to open the menu. No popups, nothing blocks, and offline it simply stays silent. And Refresh Monster DB itself is offline-safe (v1.52): it first checks that Open5e is reachable, and if it isn’t, your current database is left untouched and you’re told — clicking refresh while offline can never wipe your data.

Individual backups: you can still export each collection separately for sharing a single item — Encounters → Export All · Parties → ⬇ Export · Groups → Export · Custom Monsters → ⬇ Export · Spell Library → ⬇ Export Spells.

Device Transfer: Full Backup on the old device → transfer the file → Restore Backup on the new device.

PART 2 — RUNNING ENCOUNTERS (everything you use during a fight)

Running Combat

Where the Stat Block Opens (v1.84)

When you open a combatant's stat block, it can appear in three different places. Choose from More ▸ Stat block, which opens a short list explaining each option and remembers your choice:

  • Auto (the default) — a side panel on wide screens, and below the card on narrow ones.
  • Side panel — always beside the combatant list, whatever the screen size.
  • Below the card — always underneath the combatant, keeping the full width for your cards.
  • Pop-up — a floating window over the middle of the screen, like the spell pop-up. It doesn't block anything, so you can still damage, heal and take turns while it's open; close it with its ✕ button or the Esc key. Often the best choice on a phone or a smaller tablet, where neither of the other two has much room.

Resizing the side panel: drag the handle on its left edge to make it wider or narrower — this works with a mouse, a finger or a stylus. Double-tap or double-click the handle to return it to the default width. Your chosen width is remembered, and is automatically adjusted if you rotate a tablet or shrink the window so that both the panel and your cards always stay usable.

Moving the pop-ups (v1.87): drag a pop-up by its title bar to park it wherever suits you — with a mouse, a finger or a stylus. This works for both the stat-block pop-up and the spell pop-up. Double-click or double-tap the title bar to send it back to where it started. The stat block remembers where you put it; the spell pop-up doesn't, because it deliberately opens next to whichever spell link you tapped. Dragging is switched off on phone-sized screens, where there is nowhere useful to move a pop-up anyway.

The combatant cards now measure the space they actually have rather than the size of the screen, so they rearrange themselves neatly however wide or narrow the stat block is — on a squeezed layout the row of buttons simply moves onto its own line.

When a Condition Ends (v1.100)

Fifth edition distinguishes two very similar-sounding durations, and which one applies changes whether a creature gets to act under the effect:

  • Until the end of their next turn — the creature takes its whole turn still affected, and the condition drops as the turn finishes. This is the common case, and the default.
  • Until the start of their next turn — the condition falls away as the creature's turn begins, so they act free of it.

When you set a duration, choose which applies. The tracker then counts down at the right moment: end-of-turn conditions expire as that creature finishes, start-of-turn conditions expire before they act. Conditions set to the start of a turn are marked with a small s beside the round count, and hovering the count tells you which it is.

Repeat saving throws. Many conditions let a creature try to shake the effect off on its own turn. When you set a duration you can record the save alongside it — pick the ability (STR, DEX, CON, INT, WIS or CHA) and type the DC. The condition then carries a small CON 13 style badge, and that badge lights up and pulses when it is that creature's turn, so you are reminded to call for the roll at the right moment. The rest of the time it sits quietly out of the way.

The tracker deliberately doesn't resolve the save for you: you roll, and then remove, shorten or leave the condition as the result and the spell demand. Leave the ability blank if a condition has no repeat save.

When a condition runs out it doesn't simply vanish. It lingers for a couple of seconds, struck through and marked expired, then clears itself — so you can see what just ended and when, rather than noticing later that something is missing. This applies to both timings.

Existing conditions and older saved encounters are unaffected — anything without a choice recorded behaves as end-of-turn, exactly as before.

Passive Scores (v1.98)

AC and hit points (v1.105) can also be set here, beside each name, so a party's whole reference sheet lives in one place rather than being edited card by card. Leave them blank and the tracker keeps whatever the player's card already has. Changing them updates that player immediately — and sensibly: raising someone's maximum hit points doesn't secretly heal a wounded character, and lowering it won't leave them on more hit points than they now have.

Passive Perception, Investigation and Insight are the numbers a DM reaches for most often between rolls — who notices the ambush, who spots the lie, who sees through the disguise. You can record them per player in Party & XP, beside each name, and they then appear on that player's combatant card for reference during play.

  • Entirely optional. Leave them blank and nothing changes — the card looks exactly as it did before.
  • Fill in only what you want. If you only care about Passive Perception, set that and leave the other two empty; the card shows just the ones you've entered.
  • Saved with your party, so they come back whenever you load that party or add it to an encounter, and they travel in a Full Backup.
  • Older parties still work. Party configurations saved before this feature simply show blank boxes, ready to fill in if you want them.

This is deliberately a reference field and nothing more — the tracker isn't trying to manage character sheets, and most DMs will have those open in another tool anyway. It's here purely so the number is in front of you when a player asks "do I notice anything?".

Finding a Combatant Quickly (v1.94)

In a big fight — five players and seventeen monsters, say — the list runs to several screens, and hunting for "Skeleton 2" to apply damage means a lot of scrolling. The ☰ button beside Next Turn opens a compact list of everyone in the encounter, in initiative order, on a single screen.

  • Type to filter. Start typing a name (or an initiative number) and the list narrows as you go. On a computer the box is already focused, so you can type and press Enter to jump straight to the first match without touching the mouse.
  • Tap a name and the tracker scrolls that combatant's card to the middle of the screen and highlights it briefly, so your eye lands in the right place. The list closes itself — it is purely a way of getting somewhere, and changes nothing.
  • The dot beside each name shows condition at a glance: green for healthy, amber for bloodied, grey for defeated, and a hollow ring for an absent player. The combatant whose turn it is now is marked.
  • If the one you pick is inside a collapsed group, or hidden because you are hiding defeated combatants, the tracker reveals it for you rather than doing nothing.

Press Esc or tap elsewhere to close it without going anywhere.

Restarting, Re-rolling and Starting Over (v1.92)

Three different tools, easy to confuse, so here is exactly what each one does:

Everyone returns to full health; conditions, temporary HP, death saves, legendary and lair uses, recharges, spell slots and X/Day abilities are all restored. The round goes back to 1 with no active turn. You want to run the same fight again — testing an encounter, replaying after a wipe, or running it for a second group.
⚄ Roll Init Re-rolls initiative only. Health, conditions and ability uses are left exactly as they are. You just want a new turn order.

Restart asks for confirmation if there is anything to lose, and is undoable either way. Players marked absent stay absent — who turned up tonight isn't something the fight changed. A note is written to the combat log so the record shows where the second run began.

Rolling Initiative (v1.82)

Roll Init rolls for every monster, NPC and ally in the encounter — players keep their own rolls, and lair cards or timers keep their fixed count. Every roll is a d20 plus that creature's own initiative bonus, taken from its stat block (the initiative bonus field where the creature has one, otherwise its Dexterity modifier), so a nimble creature really does tend to go earlier.

Grouped or individual? By default, identical creatures share a single roll — five goblins all act on the same count, which is how most tables run a mob and keeps the turn order short. If you'd rather every creature rolled separately, switch it under More ▸ Initiative rolls. The menu shows which mode you're in, and your choice is remembered between sessions. Both modes always add each creature's own bonus; the mode only decides whether they share the d20.

Re-rolling part of the encounter. Tick the combatants you want — or a whole group at once using its header checkbox — and the selection bar gives you two options:

  • Reroll — re-rolls just those combatants, using whichever mode you're currently in. Everything else keeps its initiative.
  • Reroll Split — re-rolls those combatants so that each one gets its own value, whatever your default is set to. This is the quick way to break up a group: if your goblins and your wolves both landed on 14, select the goblins, split them, and the tie resolves itself.

Both are undoable, so there's no risk in trying a roll you don't like.

Initiative Housekeeping (v1.73)

Three small things that save a lot of fiddling:

  • Fix a wrong initiative — click the number in the circle on any card, type the correct value and press Enter. The list re-sorts immediately and the active turn stays with the same creature.
  • Break a tie your way — combatants on the same initiative can be nudged with the ▲ / ▼ buttons on their cards, or dragged by the handle on the left edge. Ordering only moves within the tie.
  • Move a whole group (v1.79) — when two monster groups roll the same initiative (five goblins and two wolves both on 14), the group header gets its own ORDER ▲ ▼ buttons at the right-hand end, which move that entire group above or below the other in one click. (They sit apart from the ▼ collapse arrow on the left, which expands or hides the group's members.) The arrows on individual cards still work as before for nudging a single combatant within a tie.

Damage & Healing

Enter a number in the damage field. Press Enter or click DMG. Press H or click HEAL. Temp HP absorbed first — the log shows the whole hit and how much the temp HP soaked up. Mistakes? Ctrl+Z.

Damage Types — Optional (v1.115)

Damage is untyped by default and works exactly as it always has: the number you type is the damage dealt. You never have to choose a type. It's there for the moments it matters — when the creature you're hitting resists, ignores or is vulnerable to something.

  • Where it appears — a small Type ▾ button next to DMG, but only on creatures whose stat block lists damage immunities, resistances or vulnerabilities (the same creatures that show the 🛡 Defenses pill). Everywhere else a type would change nothing, so there's no button.
  • Using it — tap Type ▾ and the picker lists that creature's own defences, grouped as Immune (no damage), Resistant (half, rounded down) and Vulnerable (double). Pick the one that matches the hit, type the damage, press DMG. The button shows what's armed — e.g. Fire ×0, Cold ½, Fire ×2.
  • One hit only — the pick applies to the next damage on that creature, then the button goes back to Type ▾. A pick you don't use clears when the turn advances. Healing ignores it.
  • Order doesn't matter — you can type the number first and pick the type after; the number stays in the box.
  • Conditional defences — lines like "bludgeoning, piercing and slashing from nonmagical attacks" are shown with their condition in italics. Choosing one is your ruling that it applies to this hit.
  • What it changes — the damage taken, the per-turn tally and the concentration DC all use the adjusted number. Immunity means no damage at all: temp HP isn't touched and no concentration check is asked for. The log records what happened, e.g. "Ice Troll took 24 fire damage — vulnerable, 12 doubled".
💡 The button follows the linked stat block, so players normally don't get one — though a druid Wild Shaped into a beast with defences does, for as long as the form lasts. If a player has resistance (a raging barbarian, say), halve the number yourself as usual.

Concentration Checks (v1.117)

When a concentrating creature takes damage, a ⚡ Concentration Check pops up with the Constitution save DC: 10 or half the damage taken (rounded down), whichever is higher. Mark it Saved, Failed (concentration ends and it's logged) or Dismiss it.

  • One attack, one save — if you enter a hit in parts (slashing, then fire, because only the fire is resisted), the parts add up in the same check: "took 15 + 7 = 22 damage", DC 11. Once you've marked the check, the next damage counts as a new hit with its own save. So for two separate attacks, resolve the first check before entering the second.
  • Nothing gets lost — when one Fireball catches two concentrating casters, their checks queue up and appear one after the other (the popup shows who's next).
  • New turn, new hit — damage entered after the turn advances always starts a fresh check, even if an old one is still open.
  • 2014 or 2024 — under the 2024 rules the DC is capped at 30. The tracker follows the 2014 / 2024 toggle in ⚔ Party & XP, which is now remembered between sessions.

Other Combatant Type

For lair actions, hazards, traps, ritual timers, reinforcements — anything needing a turn slot that isn't a creature. Teal styling. Excluded from XP and Alive counts.

An Other combatant is a full card, not a cut-down one: it has AC, hit points, damage and healing, conditions and notes, exactly like a monster. That means it covers three quite different jobs:

  • Something to destroy (v1.100) — a barred door, a barricade, a siege engine, a crystal the villain is channelling through. Give it an AC and hit points and the party can attack it like anything else: damage applies normally, the HP bar drains, and when it reaches 0 the card greys out as destroyed rather than disappearing, so you can still see what was there. Because it is an Other, smashing it awards no XP and doesn't count as a casualty.
  • An environmental effect — an avalanche, a collapsing ceiling, rising water, a spreading fire. Leave the hit points at zero; the card exists purely to claim a place in the turn order so the effect happens at the right moment each round. Put the save DC and damage in the notes field and read it straight off the card.
  • A timer — a ritual completing in three rounds, reinforcements arriving on round four, a portal closing. Set the initiative where you want it to fire and use the notes to say what happens.

One important difference: Other combatants are the only type Roll Init leaves alone. That is what makes them reliable for anything that should happen at a fixed point in the round.

How the parser reads ability names (v1.113)

Within Traits, Actions, Bonus Actions, Reactions and Legendary Actions, the parser has to work out where each ability's name ends and its description begins. Published stat blocks always put a full stop after the name, but homebrew varies, so the following are all understood:

Written asExample
Full stopMultiattack. The beast makes two attacks.
Exclamation or question markAll Hands, You Bastards! Bluemoon shouts an order.
ColonAll Hands: Bluemoon shouts an order.
Dash, with spaces around itAll Hands — Bluemoon shouts an order.
Markdown bold**All Hands.** Bluemoon shouts an order.
Name on its own lineAll Hands
Bluemoon shouts an order.

Hyphenated names such as Hit-and-Run and recharge ranges such as (Recharge 5–6) are safe, because a dash only separates a name from its description when it has spaces on both sides.

The one form that cannot be read is a name with no punctuation after it at all — All Hands Bluemoon shouts an order. There is genuinely no way to tell where the name stops and the sentence starts, so the tracker leaves it rather than guessing. If a block is written that way, add a full stop or a colon after each ability name before pasting.

If a whole section comes out empty — no Bonus Actions, say, when the block clearly has some — that is nearly always the cause: the names are formatted in a way the parser can't split. The section heading itself (Bonus Actions, Reactions, Legendary Actions) is recognised on its own, in singular or plural. Adding a full stop after each ability name and pasting again will usually fix it, and you can always add the missing entries by hand in the editor afterwards.

Nothing is ever deleted automatically: at around 80% you get a heads-up, and if you reach the limit the tracker asks you to remove a few rather than quietly dropping one. More ▸ 📷 Manage Mini Photos shows everything you have stored, what it is costing, and lets you remove any of them.
  • Photographing — any PNG, JPEG or WebP up to 12 MB. The tracker crops it square, shrinks it and stores only that small copy; your original photo is never uploaded or kept. Pictures taken on a phone keep the right way up.
  • Backups — mini photos are included in Full Backup, so they travel with everything else. Restoring with Merge keeps the photos you already have; Clear & Replace swaps in the ones from the file.

Portraits are handled privately and efficiently — the image is shrunk to a small thumbnail in your own browser and only that compact version is stored; the original file is never kept or uploaded, and nothing leaves your device. Frequently Asked Questions

How do I resolve initiative ties?

Grab the ⠿ grip icon on the left edge of any card and drag it above or below the other. Initiative values are never changed by dragging.

How do I apply AoE damage to multiple targets?

Tick checkboxes → bulk bar appears → enter damage → DMG All (full) or ½ DMG (half, for saves). Fireball example: select the targets who failed the save, DMG All; then select only those who saved, ½ DMG. Optionally set the damage type first and each creature's resistances and immunities apply (see Group Management & Bulk Actions).

How do I set HP for a monster?

Click HP ▾ on any card to open the HP mode popup: Average HP, Maximum HP, Roll Dice, or Set Value (enter any number). The Monster DB also offers these modes before adding. In the bulk action bar, Set Max HP opens the same popup — dice modes available when at least one selected combatant has a stat block.

I made a mistake — how do I fix it?

Ctrl+Z / Cmd+Z or ↩ Undo. Restores full state — HP, conditions, active turn, round — up to 20 steps back. Every state change is tracked including HP edits, AC/initiative edits, condition changes, and notes. The Undo button shows a gold count badge indicating steps remaining. New Encounter is undoable too (v1.66): if you clear the field by accident, Undo brings the whole encounter back — combatants, round, and turn. The one thing that starts fresh is the combat log, so export a session summary first if you want a permanent record.

On my phone the header and toolbar disappear when I start combat — is that normal?

Yes (v1.37). On phones and small tablets, the tracker collapses the header and toolbar into a slim bar once combat begins (and whenever you scroll down the list), so the combatant list gets nearly the whole screen. The slim bar still shows the round, whose turn it is, and a Next button. It deliberately stays collapsed while you scroll up and down — so it won't flicker in and out as you scan a long combat. To bring the full header and footer back, tap the small chevron on the left of the slim bar (or the chevron next to ? Help). On desktop and wide tablets the header always stays put.

It's a reminder only — it flags that a creature's stat block contains a start- or end-of-turn ability (regeneration, an aura, ongoing damage, a recharge-on-turn effect, and so on) and pulses during that creature's turn. It does not resolve anything, and deliberately so. One subtlety worth knowing: a "start/end of turn" effect might trigger on this creature's own turn (e.g. a troll regaining HP) or on another combatant's turn (e.g. an aura that damages anyone who starts their turn near it). The pill can't know which — it just makes sure you don't forget the effect exists. Tap it, read the ability, check the stat block, and resolve it yourself. If you realise you missed it on an earlier turn, you can apply it retroactively or use Undo to step back to that combatant and resolve it there.

How long does the tracker keep my data, and can a browser delete it?

Your encounters, parties, custom monsters, mini photos and settings live in your browser's storage on the device you use. Nothing is sent anywhere and there is no account, which is why it works offline — but it also means the browser, not the tracker, ultimately decides how long that data survives. The rules differ significantly, and one of them catches people out.

  • Add the tracker to your Home Screen (Share → Add to Home Screen) on an iPad or iPhone. Apps launched that way are exempt from the seven-day rule, and you get a full-screen app into the bargain.
  • On an Android tablet or phone you can install the tracker the same way (Chrome menu → Add to Home screen or Install app), but it is not needed to protect your data — Android's Chrome has no seven-day rule. Do it if you simply prefer a full-screen app with its own icon.
  • Take a Full Backup (More ▸ Full Backup) after any session you would mind repeating. It saves one file containing everything — encounters, parties, custom monsters and mini photos — which you can restore on any device or browser.

The tracker will also give you a gentle nudge: if you have encounters, parties, custom monsters or mini photos saved and haven't taken a backup for a while, a small amber reminder appears among the counters at the top. Tapping it takes a backup; tapping its ✕ hides it for a week. It appears sooner in Safari than elsewhere, for the reason above. If you have nothing saved yet, it never appears at all.

A Full Backup is worth taking periodically whichever browser you use. It is the only copy that is genuinely yours rather than the browser's, and it is the simplest way to move your material to a new computer, phone or tablet.

I saved the tracker to my computer — why did my encounters disappear?

Almost certainly because the file was renamed or moved. Your encounters, parties, custom monsters and settings are stored by your browser, not inside the tracker file, and the browser decides which store to use based on where the page was opened from.

When you open the tracker from a web address, that address is the same every time, so your data is always there. When you open it as a file on your computer, Safari and Firefox treat every file as a separate place — so tracker.html and tracker-v2.html each get their own, entirely separate set of saved encounters. Chrome and Edge share one store across local files, so you may not see this at all in those browsers.

If you run the tracker from a file, the rule is simple: when you update to a newer version, overwrite the existing file, keeping the same name and folder. Don't save the new version alongside the old one under a different name.

Before you change anything, take a Full Backup (More ▸ Full Backup). If data does go missing this way it isn't lost — it is still attached to the old file, so putting that file back where it was will bring it back, and you can then export a backup and restore it into the new one.

The same applies to moving the file to a different folder, or to a USB stick: to the browser that is a different place, with a different store.

Chrome, Edge and Chromebooks behave the other way round, and it is worth knowing which way you are affected. They treat every local file as the same place, so renaming or moving a downloaded copy makes no difference — your encounters simply follow. The catch is the reverse of the problem above: if you keep two copies of the tracker on the same computer, an old download and a new one, they share a single set of saved encounters. Opening the older copy and saving will overwrite what you did in the newer one, because as far as the browser is concerned they are the same tracker.

So on those browsers the advice is simply: keep one copy and delete old downloads, rather than letting several versions accumulate in your Downloads folder.

On my iPad everything looks slightly small — can I make it bigger?

Yes, and it's a Safari setting rather than anything in the tracker. On iPads, Safari loads most sites in desktop mode, which lays the page out as though the screen were wider than it really is and then shrinks the result to fit. You get more on screen — which many DMs prefer — but text and buttons end up smaller than intended. Two ways to change it, both of which the tracker fully supports:

  • Tap ＡＡ in Safari's address bar → Request Mobile Website. Safari remembers this for the tracker specifically, so you only do it once. Everything becomes larger, with bigger touch targets.
  • Add the tracker to your Home Screen (Share → Add to Home Screen). It then opens full-screen like an app, at the proper size, without Safari's address bar taking up room.

Either way, nothing is lost — your encounters, custom monsters and settings are the same. If you prefer seeing more combatants at once, simply leave it as it is; the layout adapts to whatever space it has.

How can I remember an ongoing effect on a player (e.g. recurring damage)?

Add the Other condition (end of the condition list) to that combatant with a short label like "Burning 1d6/turn", and put the details in the card's note field. The labelled pill is a visible at-a-glance reminder each round, and the note carries the specifics. This is the simplest way to track a custom recurring effect on a player or monster — the tracker reminds you it's there; you decide when and how it applies.

How do I track a transformed or polymorphed combatant?

Click ⇄ on the card. Monster DB opens in transform mode — search the new form, click ⇄ Transform. HP and AC swap. Click ↩ Revert to restore original stats.

How do I apply death saves to a boss monster?

Click ☠ DS on the monster card. It turns solid red — the monster shows death save pips at 0 HP instead of dying instantly. Clicking it on a dead monster un-dies it retroactively.

What is the Revivify countdown?

After a character dies via 3 failed death saves, a countdown shows rounds remaining before Revivify is no longer viable (10 rounds = 1 minute). Pulses red in last 3 rounds. Red bold when expired.

A player is absent tonight — how do I handle it?

Click the 👤 button in the button row at the bottom of that player's card. The card dims, shows an absent badge, and that player is skipped when you press Next Turn — but they still count toward your party size for XP and difficulty. Click 👤 Absent again when they turn up late.

How do I get a log of the encounter?

Click 📜 Log. All meaningful events are captured automatically — damage, conditions, reactions, spell slots, group drops, per-turn entries, timer expiry, undo diffs, HP changes, initiative changes, AC changes, notes, death save tracking, and legendary action use. Add DM notes at the bottom. ⬇ Export for a .txt session summary. The log resets on new encounter or when loading a saved encounter.

How do I save and resume mid-dungeon?

More → Quick Save. Next session: Encounters → load the save. Everything resumes exactly — HP, conditions, round, active turn.

What is the difference between a Party Config and an Encounter save?

Party Config: saves the player roster at full HP — use to start each session fresh. Encounter save: saves complete combat state including current HP and conditions — use to pause mid-fight or continue next session.

How do I tell if my encounter is too deadly?

⚔ Party & XP shows the difficulty rating (Easy/Medium/Hard/Deadly) in real time as monsters die. Dead monsters remain in the XP total with a strikethrough — the full encounter XP is always shown. Toggle between 2014 and 2024 rules at the top.

How do I back up my custom monsters and spells?

Custom Monsters: + Custom Monster → ⬇ Export. Custom Spells: ✦ Spell Library → ⬇ Export Spells. Re-import on any device with the matching Import buttons.

Will my data sync between devices?

No — by design. Export on old device, transfer the JSON file, Import on new device.

Where does the monster and spell data come from?

The tracker uses Open5e, an open-source community project, via its v2 API. This includes both the 2014 (SRD 5.1) and 2024 (SRD 5.2) rules, alongside some third-party open-licensed content. Monster and spell data remain subject to their original licensing and attribution requirements.

Do you support the 2024 rules?

Yes. The database now includes the 2024 SRD (5.2) monsters and spells alongside the 2014 SRD. Spells that exist in both editions show source pills so you can compare them. Note that the open SRD is a subset of the full printed 2024 Monster Manual — creatures unique to the printed book that aren't in the open SRD won't appear. For those, use ⚗ Parse Block (paste the stat block, or a Tetra-cube export) to add them yourself.
